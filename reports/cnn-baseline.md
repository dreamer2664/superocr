**Headline: the pipeline works end to end; the numbers are far from the bar,
and this report says exactly how far.** This is the from-scratch learned
reader (numpy + PIL only, no pretrained anything), trained on data generated by
`generator.py`, measured by `bench.py` (t-20261011-2f3d).

| easy | 7 | 30 | 23.33% | 98 | NO |
| hard | 11 | 180 | 6.11% | 95 | NO |

Per-glyph validation accuracy (held-out crops, both sources): **89.2%**.
Median read time per image: **0.211 s** (p95 0.248 s) — the latency budget is
not the problem; accuracy is.

## What the failures actually are (two distinct causes, not one)

1. **Per-glyph case confusions.** With ~20 glyphs per label, 89.2% per glyph
   gives 0.892^20 ≈ 10-20% per label — the easy number follows arithmetically.
   Dominant confusions are case pairs: `X→x`, `I→l`, `O→o/0`, `Z→z`, `C→c`.
   Softer augmentation (I had stroke-weight morphing at 25%, which erases
   exactly the features that tell cases apart) moved per-glyph accuracy from
   87.3% to 91.9%, and the bug below moved easy from 16.7% to 23.3%.
2. **Structural families at 0%.** `split/stitch/strippy/cut/collage/corrosion/
   cross-shadow/darts` break the *segmentation* stage before the classifier
   sees anything: letters are disjoint, doubled, or laced with lines, so box
   counts disagree with glyph counts and the reader reads garbage. No amount
   of classifier training fixes this — it is exactly the S3 jobs (t-407f,
   t-2ca7), and this report is the evidence that S3 is not optional.

A third, smaller cause: whole-phrase segmentation collapses on a few images
(e.g. `snow_000` → two boxes). Those are in the gallery, which is the point of
the gallery.

## Bugs found and fixed on the way (each one was invisible without a test)

- **`im2col` transposed after reshaping**, scrambling channel/pixel/kernel
  indices. Forward and `dw` stayed mutually consistent (both wrong together),
  so only a naive-convolution comparison caught it; `dx` was garbage.
  Now pinned by `test_conv_forward_matches_naive_reference`.
- **Polarity decided after stretching** — a stretched image is centred on
  zero, so the mean test was meaningless and the ink mask selected the
  background (98% "ink"). Thin-text images collapsed to two boxes.
- **Percentile stretch degenerate when ink is under 2% of pixels** (p2 = p98 =
  252 on a clean 640x180 sheet), which silently flattened the scale.
- **Deskew scored rotation with `expand=True` and no crop**, so the black
  expansion corners made variance grow with |angle| — the "best" angle was
  always the search limit. It looked confident and did nothing.
- **Gradient check needs float64**: in float32, finite differences drown in
  rounding noise; the check is only meaningful when the dtype flows through.

## What would move the needle (in order, for whoever picks up S2/S6)

1. **S3 structural work first** (t-407f, t-2ca7): 8 of 12 hard families fail at
   segmentation. The classifier cannot help.
2. More and better training data from `generator.py` (all 13 families), several
   thousand crops per family; the harvest is already automated and drops
   mis-segmented sheets rather than mislabelling them.
3. A bigger net and/or an ensemble — but the first two items are worth more;
   classifier capacity was not the binding constraint (8→16 channels bought
   +0.1% per glyph).
4. Confidence + fallback (t-d1a6) will not raise accuracy by itself, but it
   turns silent wrong reads into flagged ones, which is what the typer (S4)
   needs before it is let near a real text box.
